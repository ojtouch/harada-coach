# Phases 2 and 3: central goal, levels, milestones and daily service

## 2.1 Bringing out the goal

Start with the dream: "What life, what result would make you truly proud?" Then turn the dream into a goal. The method strictly separates the two: a dream is a hope, a goal is a dated commitment that you prepare for.

Advice from the method:
- Choose something that will make a real difference in your life and that makes you want to get up in the morning.
- Aim for excellence in a field: become a "craftsman", the best at something that serves yourself and others.
- In a professional setting: a goal that is good for you **and** for the organization.
- Visualize the goal as already achieved, then go back in time to reconstruct the steps that led there (the "script" of your success). See the guardrail on visualization.

If the person gets stuck:
- **50 strengths exercise**: write 50 positive things about yourself and your past achievements, draw 10 possible goals from them, show them to people close to you or to your coach, then choose one.
- Variant: write 10 possible goals and ask 3 trusted people for their opinion.

## 2.2 The two keys of the goal

Always check:
1. **Is it precious to you?** If it doesn't motivate you, it won't withstand difficulties.
2. **Is it measurable?** So you know at any moment whether you are on the right track, and so others can help concretely.

| Bad (vague) | Good (measurable and dated) |
|---|---|
| "Do my best in sales in June." | "Reach €20,000 in sales by June 30." |
| "Be a good parent." | "Have dinner with my children without screens 5 evenings out of 7 by March 31." |
| "Speak better English." | "Hold a 30-minute conversation without switching to French, validated by my teacher, by September 15." |
| "Be the best team." | "Finish first in the league standings at the end of the season." |

Useful follow-ups: "How would you know, on the day, that it's achieved?"; "What number, what observable fact?"; "What exact date?".

## 2.3 The target date

No goal without a date: "a goal without a date is just a dream". The athlete knows the day of the competition and must be at their peak that day. Set a realistic date and treat it as a commitment.

Also note the **decision date** (today): it appears at the top of the form.

The goal can be long-term (several months, a lifetime) or short-term (2 weeks, 1 month). The method recommends having both and running the whole process for each. For a first use, a 1 to 3 month goal lets you learn the method.

## 2.4 The 4 levels (same date for all)

| Level | Definition | Example (salesperson, month of June) |
|---|---|---|
| **Maximum** | Very difficult, achievable only by giving everything and with help | €16,000 (the best salesperson on the team) |
| **Intermediate** | Demanding but not extreme, often close to the maximum | €13,000 |
| **This time** | What I believe I can achieve this time with effort and the plan | €12,000 |
| **Comfort** | What I have already done and can do again, my baseline | €10,000 (last month) |

Order for filling in: maximum, then comfort, then intermediate, then this time.

Pitfalls:
- Believing the maximum is the long-term goal and the comfort level the short-term goal: **the 4 levels have the same date.**
- Setting levels at random. People who achieve their goals rely on their past data and their environment. Ask: "What is the best you have done so far? What does the best in your field do?"

Sports example from the book (national championship, 50 participants): maximum No. 1, intermediate No. 3, this time No. 8, comfort No. 30. Without the method, the student would have aimed for "No. 15" at random.

The goal chosen in the form is written as a complete sentence: "By {date}, I will have {measurable result}."

## 2.5 Milestones

People get discouraged in the middle of a goal because they can't see their progress. Set **at least 3 dated, measurable milestones** between today and the final date.

Example: goal €16,000 by June 30. Milestone 1: €5,000 by June 12. Milestone 2: €12,000 by June 22. Milestone 3: €16,000 by June 30.

Rule from the book: at each milestone, check your progress and your plans. **If you are behind, you change your actions to get back on track, you don't lower the goal.** (See the guardrail on a goal that has become unattainable.)

## Phase 3: daily service (indirect efforts)

The method distinguishes:
- **Direct efforts**: actions linked to the goal (call 15 clients, hand out 300 flyers).
- **Indirect efforts**: acts of service that build character, make you humble, strengthen relationships and give you the strength to keep going.

The person chooses **one daily service at home** and **one daily service at work** (or in their main living place). Ideally a task others avoid or don't like doing: cleaning the bathroom, tidying the shoes at the entrance, cleaning the office entrance, doing the dishes. Harada himself cleans the toilets at home and tidies his colleagues' desks.

Principles:
- You don't ask others to change: you change your own actions, and the environment changes.
- You serve without expecting anything in return.
- The book's ideal is to keep the same service for 1000 days (see guardrail on habits).

These two services go at the top of the form and at the top of the routine check sheet.

| Bad | Good |
|---|---|
| "Help at home." | "Every evening, I empty and put away the dishwasher before 9 p.m." |
| "Be nice at the office." | "When I arrive, I clean the shared coffee machine for 5 minutes." |
