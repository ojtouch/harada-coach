# Phase 4: purposes and values

## Principle

The simplest principle of the method: **purposes push you toward the goal.** Changing your habits is costly; you need a strong purpose. The deeper the purpose and the more useful it is to others as well as to yourself, the easier the goal becomes to achieve. Without purposes, you fixate on the material result and end up seeing only your own interest.

Harada recounts that at first he wanted champions for his own glory. The turning point came when he asked himself "why?": for the students, their families, the school, the neighborhood.

## The 4 perspectives

| | **For me** | **For others / society** |
|---|---|---|
| **Concrete (tangible)** | Material or measurable benefit for me: income, bonus, skill acquired, time freed up | Material benefit for others: team revenue, family comfort, better-served clients |
| **Intangible** | What I become: confidence, pride, sense of accomplishment, serenity | What others feel: my family's pride, my manager's trust, a more vibrant community |

## Process

1. Ask for the purposes box by box, starting with the one that comes most easily to the person.
2. Aim for **at least 4 to 6 lines per box**. The book's instruction is "as many lines as possible".
3. Follow-ups when a box is thin:
   - "Who else will benefit from your success? Your family, your colleagues, your clients, your community?"
   - "Who is counting on you to succeed?" (when someone depends on us, motivation rises)
   - "What will you feel on the day it's achieved?"
   - "And then, what will that allow you to do?" (dig 2 or 3 times)
4. Finally ask: "Which of these purposes would keep you going on a day of great fatigue?" Note it as the **driving purpose** in the dashboard.

## Good and bad examples

- Bad: "For me, concrete: earn more." Why: vague, no image.
- Good: "For me, concrete: pay cash for the summer trip with my parents, without credit."
- Bad: "For others, intangible: make people happy."
- Good: "For others, intangible: that my son sees me keep a difficult promise all the way through."

## Reminder

A purpose that serves only yourself is allowed, but a goal without any purpose turned toward others is fragile. If the "others" column is empty, point it out and dig deeper.
