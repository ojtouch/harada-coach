# Phase 5: analyzing yourself

## Principle

People who always get good results have good habits; the others repeat their mistakes. New habits can be learned. To do so, you must first **become aware** of the ones you have: repeat what worked, stop repeating what failed, and anticipate what could get in the way.

The analysis covers the 4 areas: **mental, skills, health, daily life**.

## Step A: past successes

"In past projects, comparable or not, what did you succeed at very well?" For each area, note the behavior that led to success.

| Area | Example |
|---|---|
| Mental | Positive thinking, relaxed, I enjoyed what I was doing |
| Skills | I followed my task list, I asked for advice, I planned in detail |
| Health | Three meals a day, 8 hours of sleep, walking 3 times a week |
| Daily life | Tidy room, time to talk with my children, no arguments |

Possible exercise: write 50 things you like about yourself and your past achievements, then look for ways to repeat them.

## Step B: past failures

Same grid, with the behaviors not to repeat.

| Area | Example |
|---|---|
| Mental | I complained, irritable, a lot of pressure |
| Skills | Worked without taking notes, without planning |
| Health | Too many meals out, not enough sleep |
| Daily life | Messy room, I took my stress out on those close to me |

## Step C: why?

For each success and each failure, ask for the **cause**: "Why did it work?", "What caused the failure?". Taking full responsibility for your past actions makes change more likely.

## Step D: upcoming obstacles and countermeasures

Predict what could get in the way, in each of the 4 areas, and write a solution for each.

| Area | Underlying problem | Countermeasure |
|---|---|---|
| Mental | I get discouraged quickly by the unexpected | I write down what weighs on me and list what needs to be done |
| Skills | I waste too much time on emails | Two fixed time slots a day for emails |
| Health | I go to bed too late | Screens off at 10:30 p.m., in bed before midnight |
| Daily life | I come home tired and don't do anything anymore | I prepare the day before what I will do when I get home |

## Coaching method

- Move forward area by area, one question at a time.
- Require observable behaviors, not character traits ("I didn't plan my week" rather than "I'm disorganized").
- **Phrase each countermeasure as an "if… then" plan**: "If {triggering situation}, then I {precise action}." This is the most effective form (see guardrails).
- Aim for at least 1 serious obstacle per area, ideally 2.
- Success behaviors and countermeasures often become **routines** in phase 8: mark them along the way.

## Good and bad examples of countermeasures

- Bad: "Obstacle: fatigue. Solution: be more motivated." Why: motivation is not an action.
- Good: "Obstacle: in the evening I no longer have the energy to practice. Countermeasure: if I haven't practiced before 7 p.m., then I do a 15-minute session right after dinner, with a timer running."
