# Phase 10 and modes B and C: the tracking rituals

The method plays out over time. Three tools: the routine check sheet (every evening), the daily diary (every day), the monthly review (every month). Plus the coach.

## Routine check sheet

### Content
- At the top: name, month, **target completion rate** (for example 85%) and achieved rate.
- Two "daily service" rows: home and work.
- Up to 10 routine rows, each with the **evaluation** column (expected effect).
- One column per day of the month (1 to 31).

### Use
- Every evening, a few seconds: **1** (or X) if the routine is done, **0** if not.
- Total per day at the bottom of the column: the day's score.
- At the end of the month: rate per routine and overall rate, then a new sheet for the following month.

### Benefits described by the method
You take back control of your life; you see the result of your work every day; you measure the effect of your actions; you forge your willpower; you gain confidence; you see the whole month on a single page; a manager sees their team members progress.

## Daily diary

### Sections (to fill in the evening, or in the morning for the previous day)
1. **Date and phrase of the day**: a phrase that keeps you on course and keeps the momentum.
2. **Top 5 tasks of the day**: including 1 or 2 drawn from the goal's 10 tasks. Mark the most important ones (the book uses a red pen).
3. **Hourly schedule**: "Plan" column (what I plan) and "Reflection" column (what actually happened), from waking up to bedtime. Tips: wake-up time and bedtime, arrival at and departure from work, lunch break, what I do between waking up and work to start well, and between work and bedtime to prepare for the next day, tasks from the 64 chart.
4. **Notes of the day, things to do later.**
5. **Self-evaluation** from 1 to 5 on 8 areas, with a comment:

| Area | Question |
|---|---|
| Overall | Did I reach my goal for the day? |
| Mental | Did I have enthusiasm and focus? |
| Body | What was my energy level (diet, exercise)? |
| Work | Did I make progress in my skills? |
| Relationships | How were my relationships with others? |
| Life | Was it a good day? |
| Learning | Did I learn something new? |
| Routines | How did my routines go? |

6. **Challenges and good things noticed today**: write as many good things as possible, with the reason for the result; include what helped you grow (helping someone, an act of service); write down the difficulties, why they were difficult, and what was tried.
7. **What would I do differently?**: "If I could redo something today, it would be…" Regret used well gives the strength not to do it again.
8. **Inspiring words, phrases, events**: anything that helped, especially in connection with the goal. When you know what you want, you notice what helps you get there.
9. **Questions for the coach.**
10. **Coach's comments.**

### Why the diary
Writing is a creative act: you write, you write more, you think deeper, ideas come, you understand yourself, you stop repeating the same mistakes. The diary lets you see yourself from the outside and be your own coach: it prepares the day (mental rehearsal), makes you prioritize, does the self-analysis, gives momentum and confidence, installs positive thinking and good habits. Between a person and their manager or mentor, it also becomes a communication channel: you write in it what is hard to say face to face, and the mentor answers in writing.

### The coach's role in diary mode
After the sections, write feedback in 3 to 5 lines: one thing done well and why, one thing to adjust tomorrow, one reflection question. Believe in the person's success and show it.

## Monthly review

Once a month, go back over the long-term form, the 64 chart and the routine check sheet:
1. **Routines**: rate per routine and overall against the target rate. Which ones have become automatic? Which ones resist, and why?
2. **Milestones and levels**: where am I compared with the planned milestone and the 4 levels?
3. **Tasks**: check off the completed tasks in the chart, choose new ones to get back to 10.
4. **Next month's routines**: keep, remove, add. Write the new sheet.
5. **Gap**: if behind, change the actions and the plan to get back on track (the book's rule: do not lower the goal). Then apply the guardrail on the unreachable goal if the gap is structural.
6. **Lessons of the month**: 3 successes and their causes, 3 difficulties and their countermeasures (analysis in the 4 areas).
7. **Goal reached?** Celebrate it, thank those who helped, then set the next one by running the process again.

The method recommends having in parallel a long-term goal (which gives lasting meaning) and a short-term goal (the coming month or two), each with the full process.

## The coach

What a coach does according to the method:
- They support, inspire, guide, encourage and bring out the best. They are an agent of change.
- They see the person often (every day if possible at first) to check they stay on track, and keep the course toward the ideal state.
- They ask questions related to the goal, help remove obstacles and overcome resistance to change.
- They observe habits, routines and improvement efforts.
- They help the vision emerge when the person cannot picture their future.
- They share the responsibility of avoiding failure: they work together.

Difference between teaching and coaching: teaching leads someone to the right answer and brings knowledge; coaching draws the answer out of the person and has them apply what they know. Teaching suits beginners, coaching helps people progress in practice.

Practical posture tips taken from the book (to be taken as relationship advice, not guaranteed techniques): sit side by side or at 90° rather than face to face, keep a respectful distance, show signs of listening (nodding, smiling, making eye contact), believe the person will succeed, match their pace of speech, reuse their own words to show you understood.

Harada was able to coach 600 students thanks to the forms: he read them and answered in red ink, back and forth. The form becomes the student's personal coach, and the student learns to wear the coach's hat themselves.
