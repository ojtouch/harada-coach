# The mind: the PCDSS cycle and 5 ways to build your mind

To be used during phase 9 (phrase of the day, character routines) and in diary and review modes when the person gets discouraged.

## The PCDSS cycle

Harada adapted Deming's PDCA cycle (designed for business problems) to the development of people.

| Step | Meaning | Method tool |
|---|---|---|
| **Plan**: think and write | Set objective, concrete, demanding goals, with a date | Long-term form |
| **Check**: check your attitude | Keep a positive attitude. Start by tidying and cleaning your living and working space, serving others. If your attitude drops: repeat your phrases or call on a support | Daily service, phrases |
| **Do**: act consistently | Repeat the actions that bring you closer to the goal, no matter what. Endurance is essential | Routine check sheet |
| **See**: master yourself | See every day what helps and what hurts, adjust your actions, learn from your failures | Daily diary |
| **Share**: share | Pass on your experiences and knowledge: individual skill becomes collective skill | Exchanges, mentoring |

The diary works on 4 aspects of the "self": **self-awareness** (what motivates me, how I work best), **self-control** (consciously repeating good behaviors), **self-esteem** (competence + self-respect, which comes from integrity) and **self-image** (which grows with confidence).

## The 5 ways to build your mind

1. **Use your mind: think and write.** Writing is the best way to think clearly. Someone who writes regularly thinks regularly. Distinguish "using your mind" from "being used by your mind": do not let negative thoughts take control (reframing such as "cancel, cancel").
2. **Clean your mind: gratitude.** Develop gratitude and humility, in particular through service and cleaning. Acts of responsibility and saying "thank you" clean the mind.
3. **Strengthen your heart: perseverance.** Determination does not only come from hardships overcome: it comes above all from continuous improvement, every day, without a break. Believe deeply in yourself. The ego is the main enemy. Question to ask: "Are you the hero of your own life?"
4. **Organize your heart: step back from your failures.** Look at yourself in the third person to detach from a past failure that sticks to your mind. Mistakes are how we learn (like the child who falls while learning to walk). You cannot erase a failure, but you can stop regretting it and not repeat it. The diary and planning help separate the past from the future.
5. **Expand your heart: thank and share.** Thank colleagues, managers and loved ones through words and attitude. Someone who says they achieved everything alone confuses self-reliance with selfishness. Share your knowledge with others: "one plus one equals three". The "jiri-rita" spirit: my interest and that of others are one.

## Change and resistance

The force that prevents change is **resistance**: you say you want to change, then you go back to doing what you did before. Common excuses: "it's not the right time", "it's not in the budget", the fear of making a mistake. The first step is to decide to change; the method then provides the path. The coach spots and names resistance gently when it appears ("I notice we've postponed this action for the third time. What's going on?").

## The book's formula for success

Dream of what you are capable of doing, set a clear goal to be the best at something, work while believing in yourself, plan, persevere and never give up.
